02 / THE BRIEF

Keep the form.
Make it move.

Inspired by the Ghost from Destiny, I designed four articulated spikes, a rotating rear shell, and expressive lighting around a 100 mm main-sphere limit.

Preserving its proportions drove the design. Additional motion axes were dropped when their mechanisms competed with the servos, boards, and wiring for space.

03 / ARCHITECTURE

Separate power.
Shared control.

Two 5 V inputs supply control and actuation. All outputs share the main board’s ground reference.

04 / MECHANICS

Four spikes.
One repeated principle.

Rotation becomes articulation.

The servo-driven cam pushes a follower integrated into the spike. As the cam radius changes, the spike pivots around its hinge.

A preloaded torsion spring keeps the follower against the cam, allowing all four spikes to return regardless of orientation.

Cam Spring Spike / follower

28.62–41 mm cam diameter across a half-turn.
CAD geometry, not measured spike travel.

Designed around the axes.

The servo housing locates the mechanisms. A shared central opening routes wires forward through the main PCB.

Balance support and friction.

The rear servo supports and rotates the shell. Light contact with the front shell restrains wobble; excess preload adds drag.

05 / ELECTRONICS

All five servos and the LED board operated together. Total system current remains unmeasured.

46 × 46 MM / 20 LEDS

Expression through light.

Individual resistors limit LED current. A single PWM-controlled MOSFET produces breathing and motion-synchronized brightness.

06 / CONTROL

Choreography,
one channel at a time.

Direct LEDC outputs drive five servos at 50 Hz and lighting at 5 kHz. Interpolation softens transitions; keyframes coordinate the performance.

07 / REVISIONS

What testing changed.

01

A shared envelope.

Cams, servo axes, and the PCB competed for space. Iterating the housing and board outline together produced a stack that fits the original shell.

02

Gravity was not enough.

The unsprung lower spike stayed open. Preloaded torsion springs made return independent of orientation.

03

Light replaced the LCD.

The circular display never produced a usable image. A custom LED face preserved expression with a simpler interface—and became a stronger visual result.

THE MAIN LESSON

Design the interfaces
with the parts.

Actuator axes, board outlines, wire paths, and assembly access shaped the system together. Settling those interfaces early matters more than optimizing each part in isolation.

Final mass, usable interior volume, and measured spike travel remain open.
