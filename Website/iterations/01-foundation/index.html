03 / PROJECT BRIEF

Character first.
Every millimeter counts.

The central challenge was preserving the Ghost’s proportions while fitting an entire moving system inside.

Inspired by the Ghost from Destiny, I wanted to give a familiar static form physical expression: four independently articulated spikes, a rotating rear shell, and a face that responds through light.

I designed the CAD, both custom PCBs, and all device firmware, then assembled and tested the integrated system. The existing Ghost Alexa device was an inspiration; none of its code or hardware was reused. Connected-assistant functionality was outside the scope.

The main sphere had a maximum outside-diameter target of 100 mm. Inside, the servos, cams, boards, and wires compete for an approximately 94 mm diameter envelope. Front rotation and moving rear spikes were considered, then set aside to preserve the intended scale.

04 / SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

One controller.
A coordinated system.

Separate power paths supply control and actuation. A shared ground gives all six PWM outputs a common reference.

01

Control power

Micro-USB provides programming and 5 V input. An AP2112K regulator supplies 3.3 V to the ESP32-S3 module on the main board.

02

Actuator power

A separate USB-C 5 V input supplies the servos and LEDs. Bulk and local capacitance support the combined load.

03

Shared reference

The power paths share the main PCB’s ground plane. Five PWM channels command motion; a sixth controls front lighting.

05 / MECHANICAL DESIGN

Small mechanisms.
Expressive movement.

Four cam-driven spikes surround a shared servo housing. A fifth servo directly drives the rear shell.

A rotating cam.
A spring-loaded return.

The follower is integrated into the printed spike. As the servo turns a cam with increasing radius, contact pushes the spike around its hinge. A torsion spring biases the follower toward the cam and returns the spike as the radius decreases.

The spring matters because the four mechanisms face different directions. Gravity can help the upper spike close while leaving the lower one open. Preloaded springs make the return independent of orientation.

Servo-driven cam Torsion spring Integrated spike / follower

The revised CAD cam increases from 28.62 to 41 mm in diameter across a half-turn. These are geometry values, not measured spike travel.

Packaging is part of the mechanism.

Servo axes dictate the internal housing, even though the exterior looks symmetric. Wires pass through aligned openings in the housing and main board to reach the front-side connectors.

Support within a fixed envelope.

The rear servo supplies rotation and primary support. Light contact against the front shell reduces wobble, but too much preload adds friction. The final geometry revision still needs validation.

06 / CUSTOM ELECTRONICS

The boards follow
the body.

Custom geometry made the electronics fit the mechanisms, the cable routes, and the front-light arrangement.

01 / PROFILE

Clearance designed in.

Four edge cutouts make space for cam travel. The central opening routes servo leads forward, and bottom-facing USB connectors stay accessible.

02 / STACKUP

Four layers, tight packaging.

Outer layers carry components and routing, supported by internal 5 V and ground fills. Component heights were checked against the servo housing.

03 / BRING-UP

Tested as a combined load.

Both boards and all five servos were connected together to verify concurrent PWM control and lighting. Total system current has not been measured.

46 × 46 MM / 20 BLUE LEDS

A face made of light.

Each LED has its own current-limiting resistor. A low-side MOSFET converts a single PWM signal into breathing and performance-synchronized brightness. A three-wire connection carries power, ground, and control from the main board.

07 / EMBEDDED CONTROL

Six channels.
One performance.

Direct ESP32-S3 LEDC control turns independent outputs into synchronized, staggered, and paired movement.

Early servo-library tests showed unreliable multi-channel behavior. Switching to direct LEDC channels provided independent control; the underlying library failure was not isolated. Five servo outputs run at 50 Hz, while lighting uses a separate 5 kHz channel.

The routine is open loop: it relies on mechanical indexing and verified travel, with no position feedback. Time-based interpolation smooths transitions, and keyframes coordinate the spike pairs, rear-shell angle, and LED brightness.

CONTROL BOUNDARY A fixed demonstration sequence. No sensor feedback, wireless behavior, or runtime user input.

08 / INTEGRATION & REVISIONS

Where the design
met the hardware.

The most useful changes came from testing how the subsystems behaved together.

01

PACKAGING

Make the stack fit.

The cams, servo axes, PCB, and wiring all needed the same limited space. The housing evolved around the required output axes while the main board gained mechanism cutouts and a central wire path.

RESULT Five servos, four cam mechanisms, and both boards fit within the intended shell.

02

MECHANICAL RETURN

Gravity is not symmetric.

Without springs, the upper spike returned with gravity’s help while the lower spike stayed open. Adding a preloaded torsion spring at each hinge kept every follower biased toward its cam.

RESULT All four spikes follow their cams and return closed regardless of orientation.

03

DISPLAY PIVOT

Simplify the face.
Keep the expression.

The circular LCD never produced a usable image: tests showed partial RGB output or a line of color. With the cause unresolved, I replaced it with a custom LED board and a simpler PWM interface.

RESULT Breathing and coordinated lighting became one of the build’s strongest visual features.

A SMALL RELIABILITY FIX

A fractured LED-board wire exposed an assembly strain problem. Resoldering and hot-glue strain relief have survived multiple subsequent assembly and test cycles.

WHAT I WOULD CARRY FORWARD

Design the interfaces
as early as the parts.

The most important constraint was shared space. Defining actuator axes, the PCB envelope, connector access, and wiring routes early made the later design decisions clearer.

This was my first PCB-design project. It reinforced the value of establishing the board outline and checking component availability before detailed routing.

On the mechanical side, symmetric motion did not require symmetric packaging. On the control side, accurate indexing mattered just as much as the numerical servo commands.

10 / FUTURE IMPROVEMENTS

Refine what works.

A focused path toward a more polished build, separate from the remaining validation work.

01 / MECHANICAL

Broader follower contact

A small contact-area increase could improve cam engagement while preserving the mechanism and its clearances.

02 / ELECTRONICS

A more serviceable board

A future revision could formalize cable strain relief and revisit startup-sensitive GPIO choices after an audit.

03 / SOFTWARE

Reusable character actions

Named behaviors such as talking and thinking could reuse the existing motion helpers beyond a fixed timeline.

Painting, hidden springs, alternative displays, and connected-assistant features remain optional longer-term ideas.
